🔬 CHILD DEVELOPMENT RESEARCH LAB
Study & approachUnderstanding child development

Secondary research mapping how children aged 0–5 grow across the motor, cognitive, social and emotional domains, and how these domains work together in the first five years.

🏃 MOTOR LAB
Motor lab
🏃What is it?

Development of a child's musculoskeletal system and their ability to touch and physically explore their surroundings successfully.

Motor lab · 1 of 2
💪Gross motor
  • Develops from core muscle strength
  • Big movements: sitting, crawling, walking, jumping
Motor lab · 2 of 2
✋Fine motor
  • Control of smaller muscles: thumb, hand, shoulder
  • For refined, precise actions
🧠 COGNITIVE LAB
Cognitive lab
🧠What is it?

The brain's core functions: thinking, reading, learning, remembering and paying attention, used to solve problems and make decisions.

Piaget's theory
💡Piaget

A child's intelligence evolves as they grow. Children construct a mental model of the world.

4 stages of intellectual development
  • Infancy: Sensorimotor
  • Early childhood: Pre-operational
  • Elementary & early teens: Concrete operational
  • Adolescence & adulthood: Formal operational
🤝 SOCIO-EMOTIONAL LAB
Socio-emotional lab
🤝What is it?

How children learn to understand, experience, express and manage emotions, and build meaningful relationships with others.

Kohlberg's theory
⚖️Kohlberg

Moral development grows in three primary levels, and not everyone reaches the highest stage.

3 levels of moral development
  • Preconventional morality
  • Conventional morality
  • Postconventional morality
Developmental milestonesRise through the milestones

Children reach milestones in how they play, learn, speak, act and move. Each child develops at their own pace, but milestones show the changes to expect.

Primary research · play testing

Rural & semi-urban visits

We visited a preschool and a village, and play-tested our cardboard explorations with children on campus. Watching children with the toys showed us their world, their ergonomics and their physical interactions.

It also broke some of our adult assumptions about how children react.

Some of my explorations

Simple yet powerful

We explored many ideas with the activity cards, but the toys had to be easy enough for caregivers and parents to make effortlessly.

The final toys come with simple instructions. They can be torn out by hand without tools and joined without glue or tape.

Final toy · Social-emotional + motor

Human Box

Three stacked boxes: one holds faces and expressions, one holds bodies, one holds legs. A child rotates each box to build any person they can imagine, or stacks the boxes into a tower.
